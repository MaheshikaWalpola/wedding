VisaElectronic Travel Authorisation (ETA): German and Indian passport holders need an ETA before flying: a free online pre-approval linked to your passport. Without it airlines can refuse boarding. Apply only at the official site, eta.gov.lk (look-alike sites charge fake fees): choose Apply as Individual, then Tourist ETA. Approval comes by email in one to three days; apply two to three weeks ahead and save or print it. Your passport must be valid for six months or more. Other nationalities: check the same site.

At the wedding dinner

One plate, many trips to the buffet

A small Sri Lankan custom, easy to miss, that helps us more than you might think.

At a Sri Lankan hotel wedding, the kitchen does not count guests by the list we give them. It counts the main dinner plates that come back at the end of the night, and that number is what we are charged for. So every extra plate looks, to the hotel, like an extra guest.

  • Keep your one main plate for the whole dinner. Go back to the buffet as often as you like with it: the rice and curry are meant to be enjoyed in rounds.
  • Would you like a clean plate? Just ask any waiter and they will bring you a fresh one. Please do not take a new plate from the stack at the buffet.
  • The small plate beside your main plate is for bones, shells and anything you would rather set aside, so your main plate stays yours.
  • Dessert glasses, side plates and drinks are not counted. Help yourself to as many as you wish.

Thank you for indulging us in this. It is the quiet way the hotel keeps its count, and it lets us feed everyone generously without a surprise at the end.

Good to know

Getting around and local tips

Transport

  • PickMe, the local ride app, like Uber. Cheap and reliable; get it before you arrive. Good for long rides too: a car with a driver for a day, a few days, or a trip across the island, booked in the app with the fare shown before you confirm.
  • Uber works in Colombo and on the west coast.
  • Tuk-tuks are everywhere. Book one through PickMe for a fixed fare, or agree the price first.
  • Car and driver for the whole stay is the easiest way to tour: see People we know.
  • Trains are scenic but slow.
  • Driving yourself? Message us, we can help.

Money

The currency is the Rupee (LKR). Carry cash: many tuk-tuks, shops and temples are cash only. ATMs are in towns; cards work at hotels. Tip a little at restaurants when there is no service charge, round up tuk-tuks, and tip drivers and guides.

Staying connected

Buy a tourist SIM or eSIM at the airport (Dialog or Mobitel). Everyone uses WhatsApp. Plugs are the three-pin Type G and Type D, 230 V, so bring an adapter.

Mosquitoes

There are mosquitoes all year, busiest in the early morning and the late afternoon, roughly 5 to 7 PM, and after rain, and dengue does the rounds on the coast, so a repellent is the one thing to pack. From home: Anti Brumm, Autan or any spray with DEET or icaridin. In Sri Lanka every supermarket (Cargills Food City, Keells, Arpico) and pharmacy sells them cheaply: Odomos cream, Soffell spray, mosquito coils and plug-in vaporisers for the room. Long sleeves and trousers in the evening help, and hotels near the venue have air conditioning and screens. The dengue mosquito bites in daylight, so use it in the daytime as well, not only at night.

Vaccinations and health

No vaccination is required to enter Sri Lanka from Europe or India; only travellers arriving from a yellow-fever country need a yellow-fever certificate. Sri Lanka has been malaria-free since 2016, so no malaria tablets. Travel clinics usually suggest routine vaccines up to date plus hepatitis A, and typhoid for longer or rural stays. Dengue vaccine: one exists (Qdenga). In Germany the official advice recommends it only for people who have had dengue before, and the two doses are three months apart, so a full course is no longer possible before December; some travel doctors give a single dose anyway, so ask yours. In India it was approved in July 2026 but will not be on sale until 2027. For most of us, repellent is the protection. See your doctor or a travel clinic (in Germany, the Tropeninstitut or your Hausarzt) four to six weeks before you fly. Drink bottled water, and do not pet stray dogs.

Temples and etiquette

Cover shoulders and knees, remove shoes and hats, carry a scarf. Never turn your back to a Buddha statue for a photo.

Be a little careful

Some drivers steer you to gem or spice shops on commission; decline. Agree every price upfront. Swim only where there are flags or lifeguards; currents can be strong. Monkeys grab food, phones and sunglasses at temples and Sigiriya. Drink bottled water, use repellent, wear SPF 50 or higher, and watch belongings in crowds.

Booking a hotel

Our advice: book on the hotel's own website whenever you can, and read the Google reviews first; Booking.com is fine when the hotel has no site of its own. Choose a hotel over an Airbnb: a hotel has staff around the clock and someone to call if anything goes wrong. Check the current rating and price when you book.

Food and shopping

Try rice and curry, hoppers and string hoppers, kottu roti, fresh seafood, curd and treacle, king coconut and the short eats. Bring home Ceylon tea (we drink Dilmah), cinnamon from Cinnamon Lavish and other spices, batik and linen, Ayurveda products from Siddhalepa or Spa Ceylon, and sapphires only from trusted shops.

Things to do

Close to the venue, and across the island

The first cards are an easy afternoon from Kalutara; the rest is by city, for anyone staying on.

Around the wedding

Kalutara beach and the sunset, a Kalu Ganga boat ride, the Kalutara Bodhiya temple, Richmond Castle, and Brief Garden by Bevis Bawa.

Bentota and Kosgoda

25 minutes south. Bentota beach for swimming and water sports, and the Kosgoda turtle hatchery.

Galle Fort

About an hour. A Dutch colonial fort by the sea: the ramparts, the lighthouse and clock tower, boutiques and cafés inside the fort. Land inside the walls is now among the most expensive in Sri Lanka, over 20 million rupees a perch, since so many of the old houses were bought by foreigners.

Ayurveda and wellness

Siddhalepa Ayurveda, the island's best-known Ayurveda name: the hospital and spa in Mount Lavinia just south of Colombo, and the doctor-led health resort in Wadduwa near the venue. Spa Ceylon in Colombo and Galle Fort (day spa and shops). In Kandy, Hill City Retreat on Bowalawatta Road: authentic Ayurveda treatments from a facial or head massage (USD 30 to 35) to the full body massage, shirodhara and herbal bath (USD 100), +94 77 081 1562. The Anantara Spa at the venue is lovely and expensive.

Colombo

Port City, the new district on reclaimed land, is where the fun is: karting at SpeedBay, a golf driving range on the sea, ATV rides on the dunes, a sunset cruise from the marina, the casino, duty-free shopping for visitors (collected at the airport when you leave), and the best sunset and view of the city. Nearby, the Dutch Hospital by the World Trade Centre has the cafés, bars and night life.

Gangaramaya Temple and Seema Malaka on the lake, Galle Face Green at sunset, Independence Square, Viharamahadevi Park, the Colombo National Museum for the history, the Lotus Tower (about USD 20 a person for the observation deck and the digital art museum).

Shopping and food: One Galle Face, Cinnamon Life, Colombo City Centre and Havelock City Mall; tea and a light meal at Dilmah Urban Estate at One Galle Face; and try at least one five-star buffet, at ITC Ratnadipa, Cinnamon Life or the Hilton.

Kandy

The Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, the island's holiest Buddhist shrine (light-coloured clothing, shoulders and knees covered), Kandy Lake, the Kandy View Point, a Kandyan dance show, and the Royal Botanical Gardens at Peradeniya. The Ambuluwawa Tower is a detour of about an hour each way and a narrow spiral climb, better for a small group.

For the history, Ehelepola Walawwa, a noble house of the last Kandyan kings that the British turned into a prison: since 2025 the island's first wax museum, the old cells turned into a shopping arcade, and at the King's Court a dinner served the way the royal court ate, about LKR 12,000 a person, reserve ahead. For a treatment, Hill City Retreat (see Ayurveda and wellness). To stay: Kandy Myst by Cinnamon, new in 2025 with a rooftop pool over the hills.

Nuwara Eliya

Tea country and colonial charm. Gregory Lake, a factory tour and tasting at Pedro Tea Estate (about LKR 500 to 1,000 a person), Horton Plains with World's End at dawn, and the waterfalls at Lover's Leap and Bambarakanda. Adam's Peak, a dawn pilgrimage climb, is in season from December. Pack a warm layer: 10 to 15 degrees at night.

Ella

Take the morning train from Nanu Oya to Ella, one of the great rides, in that direction. Then the Nine Arch Bridge, Little Adam's Peak (a gentle hike), Ella Rock for the keen, Ravana Falls, Diyaluma Falls, and Lipton's Seat if time permits. For an afternoon off, the Ravana Pool Club, a pool bar above the valley.

South coast

Whale and dolphin watching from Mirissa (November to April) and its beach bars and Coconut Tree Hill, snorkelling, diving and turtles at Hikkaduwa, diving and kayaking at Unawatuna, surfing at Weligama (good for beginners), Jungle Beach and the Japanese Peace Pagoda at Unawatuna, and the beaches at Unawatuna and Ahangama.

Yala and the safari parks

A dawn safari in Yala (5:30 AM pick-up; leopards and elephants, and a jeep we can recommend), then the Kataragama Temple. Udawalawe for elephants (easy, reliable, good with families), Minneriya for the elephant gathering, paired with Sigiriya. Wilpattu is quiet with fewer jeeps; Kumana is remote, for longer trips.

Waterfalls

Bambarakanda, the island's tallest, and Diyaluma near Ella with its natural pools at the top; Ravana Falls right by the Ella road; St. Clair's and Devon Falls seen from the road near Talawakelle on the way to Nuwara Eliya; Lover's Leap above Nuwara Eliya; Dunhinda near Badulla; and Bopath Ella, shaped like a bo leaf, near Ratnapura. These are the famous ones; the island has hundreds more.

Tea, from bush to cup

A factory tour and tasting at Pedro Tea Estate or the Damro Tea Centre (the former Labookellie) near Nuwara Eliya, and the Ceylon Tea Museum in Kandy. In Colombo, Dilmah Urban Estate at One Galle Face, opened in 2026, is the place to taste and buy: single-estate brews, iced teas and tea cocktails, and the whole Dilmah range to take home.

Cultural triangle

Sigiriya, the Lion Rock with its frescoes and huge views (climb at dawn; for the sunrise or sunset over Sigiriya itself, climb Pidurangala, the rock opposite), the Dambulla cave temple with its murals and rows of Buddha statues, and the ancient royal cities of Polonnaruwa and Anuradhapura, best by bicycle. We do not recommend the Pinnawala elephant orphanage; the animals are not well treated there.

Where to stay

From the grandest to the most sensible

For the wedding, stay near the venue: Kalutara, Wadduwa, Waskaduwa, Bentota or Beruwala. The first four names are the closest. Where a hotel has an official Sri Lanka Tourism star grade, that is the group it is in; the boutique and luxury resorts are mostly ungraded and sit by price and reputation.
